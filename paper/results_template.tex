\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Benchmark.}  We regenerated the architecture sweep for this version.  It contains @@n_archs@@ CIFAR-10 networks:
@@nt_mlp@@ MLPs, @@nt_cnn@@ CNNs, @@nt_dws_cnn@@ depthwise-separable CNNs, @@nt_resnet@@ ResNet-style and @@nt_dws_resnet@@ depthwise ResNet-style networks,
each with ReLU, $x^2$, $x^3{+}x$ or $x^2{+}x$ activations (@@na_relu@@, @@na_x2@@, @@na_x3px@@ and @@na_x2px@@ networks) and no normalization, LayerNorm or GroupNorm.
CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-16-120 results from an earlier version are withdrawn: the underlying files could not be recovered
and the CIFAR-100 ReLU subset had only three networks.

\textbf{Training.}  30-epoch SGD (momentum $0.9$, weight decay $5{\times}10^{-4}$, cosine schedule, batch $128$, random crop with padding $4$
and horizontal flip), learning rate $0.1$, or $0.01$ with gradient clipping $1.0$ for polynomial networks without normalization.  The sweep is
run by \texttt{sweep\_gpu.py}, which follows this recipe with two differences from the earlier CPU pipeline: the dataset and the augmentation live on the GPU, and the random-crop padding
adds zeros in normalized space, whereas the earlier pipeline padded raw images with black pixels (which normalize to a nonzero constant).  The two pipelines are therefore not identical, and
accuracies are not comparable across them: for the @@shared_n@@ architectures shared with the earlier version the two-seed sweep mean is higher than the earlier three-seed 30-epoch mean for
@@shared_n_higher@@ of @@shared_n@@ (by @@shared_diff_min@@ to @@shared_diff_max@@ points; @@shared_ln_mlp@@ for the LayerNorm $x^2{+}x$ MLP).  All comparisons in this paper use the new sweep only.  Every architecture is trained with two seeds (0 and 1).  Zero-cost proxies and the cost estimate are computed
once, at the seed-0 initialization.  A network is called \emph{failed} if training diverged or its mean test accuracy is below $15\%$.

\textbf{ezkl.}  All calibrations in this section use ezkl 9.5.0 (\texttt{calibrate\_settings}, \texttt{target=resources}), $20$ real CIFAR-10 test images as calibration
inputs, and the seed-0 \emph{trained} weights, on a 24-logical-core AMD Threadripper PRO 3945WX host shared with other jobs.  One rule decides which networks enter every calibration-based analysis: networks whose seed-0 training failed (nothing meaningful to calibrate)
and networks whose estimated $\logrows$ exceeds $25$ (irrelevant to the budgets studied) are excluded.  @@calib_records@@ networks were calibrated; @@calib_dropped@@ of them met the exclusion rule (they were calibrated in an early pass
before the rule was applied) and are dropped, leaving @@calib_used@@, of which @@calib_failed_used@@ failed inside ezkl.  The dropped and skipped lists are released.

\textbf{Released data.}  Per-architecture results for both seeds (accuracy, proxies at initialization, cost estimate), the calibration records with the per-operator
features used for fitting, and every summary number in this section are in the repository, together with the scripts that regenerate Tables~\ref{tab:corr}--\ref{tab:ezkl}.
Trained network weights (about 0.7 GB) are not released.

\subsection{NASWOT}

Standard NASWOT scores the outputs of \texttt{nn.ReLU} modules.  It returns $-\infty$ on all @@naswot_inf_poly@@ of the @@n_poly@@ polynomial networks because they contain none; for the $x^2$ networks the degeneracy
is also mathematically correct (Proposition~\ref{prop:naswot}), but the count does not test the proposition.  To remove the confound we score the sign of each activation module's input instead
(Section~\ref{sec:theory}).  That variant equals the standard score on ReLU networks ($\rho=@@A_naswot_sign_relu@@$) and is defined for every polynomial network, yet it is negatively correlated with accuracy there
($x^2$ @@A_naswot_sign_x2@@, $x^3{+}x$ @@A_naswot_sign_x3px@@, $x^2{+}x$ @@A_naswot_sign_x2px@@; Table~\ref{tab:corr}).  The failure of NASWOT on polynomial networks is
therefore not only a missing-module artifact.

\subsection{Budget-Constrained Architecture Search}

\textbf{Protocol.}  The pool is the @@n_pool@@ calibrated trained networks; for a budget $B$ it contains those with calibrated $\logrows\le B$.  A method selects $K=@@K@@$ networks to train, and the outcome is the best mean accuracy among them.
The joint proxy's cost term is ezkl's \emph{calibrated} assigned cells, an oracle cost that is at least as informative as the model's estimate, so the comparison favors the joint proxy.
Each of @@splits@@ random $50/50$ splits of the pool tunes $\lambda$ over $\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2,4\}$ for the joint proxy on one half (strict improvement is required to move away from a smaller $\lambda$, so ties go to $\lambda=0$, i.e.\ NEAR-only) and evaluates all methods on the other.  Table~\ref{tab:search} reports regret against the best network in the evaluation half.
Failed networks are not in the pool, so no method can be penalized for selecting one; this is a limitation.

\textbf{Result.}  Under calibrated budgets the tuned joint proxy is indistinguishable from NEAR-only: $\lambda=0$ is chosen in @@lam0_pct@@\% of tuning splits, so the two select the same networks, and Joint ties NEAR-only in almost
every split at $K=@@K@@$ (win/tie/loss against NEAR-only: @@jvn20@@, @@jvn21@@, @@jvn22@@, @@jvn23@@ at $B=20,21,22,23$).  Because ties favor $\lambda=0$ by construction, we also report the untuned test (Table~\ref{tab:lambda}): the mean regret of each fixed $\lambda$ on the evaluation halves is at its minimum at $\lambda=$ @@lam_best20@@, @@lam_best21@@, @@lam_best22@@, @@lam_best23@@ for $B=20,\dots,23$, with regret @@lam_reg0_20@@, @@lam_reg0_21@@, @@lam_reg0_22@@, @@lam_reg0_23@@ at $\lambda=0$ against @@lam_regmin_20@@, @@lam_regmin_21@@, @@lam_regmin_22@@, @@lam_regmin_23@@ at the best fixed $\lambda$.  We therefore withdraw the earlier claim that the joint proxy improves on NEAR-only.
NEAR-only does beat random selection by a wide margin at every budget (regret @@reg20_near_only@@ vs.\ @@reg20_random@@ at $B=20$, @@reg21_near_only@@ vs.\ @@reg21_random@@ at $21$, @@reg22_near_only@@ vs.\ @@reg22_random@@ at $22$ and

The pools are mixed: ReLU networks are @@relu_share_min@@--@@relu_share_max@@\% of each, and among the $K=@@K@@$ highest-NEAR networks at $B=20,\dots,23$ there are @@neartop20_poly@@, @@neartop21_poly@@, @@neartop22_poly@@ and @@neartop23_poly@@ polynomial

\textbf{Why $\lceil\log_2 C\rceil$ is not always $\logrows$.}  In ezkl's settings the reported row count is half of the assigned cells: the ratio of assigned cells to rows lies between @@g_ratio_min@@ and @@g_ratio_max@@ in all @@g_ratio_n@@ calibrated networks.  The calibrated $\logrows$ is then
the largest of four resource requirements, not a function of rows alone: $\logrows=\max(\lceil\log_2\text{rows}\rceil,\lceil\log_2\text{const}\rceil,\lceil\log_2\text{lookup span}\rceil,\lceil\log_2\text{range-check span}\rceil)$,
where \emph{const} is ezkl's \texttt{total\_const\_size} (weights stored as constants: for MLPs it equals the parameter count, median const/parameters @@g_cp_mlp@@, while for convolutional networks it is @@g_cp_conv_min@@--@@g_cp_conv_max@@ times larger; we did not investigate why), \emph{lookup span} is the width of the calibrated lookup range and \emph{range-check span} is the largest range-check table.
The rule reproduces the calibrated $\logrows$ exactly for @@g_exact@@ of @@g_n@@ networks (Table~\ref{tab:accounting}; ReLU @@g_relu@@, $x^2$ @@g_x2@@, $x^2{+}x$ @@g_x2px@@, $x^3{+}x$ @@g_x3px@@).
The resource that binds is the row count in @@g_bind_rows@@ networks, the constants in @@g_bind_const@@, the lookup table in @@g_bind_lookup@@ and a range-check table in @@g_bind_range_check@@.  This explains the $x^3{+}x$ anomaly: @@g_bigrc_x3px@@ of the @@g_x3_n@@ dumped $x^3{+}x$ networks have a range check of
$\pm2^{21}$ (a table of $2^{22}$ entries, with ezkl's calibration selecting the lower input scale $11$), which forces $\logrows=23$ regardless of cell count, and none of the @@g_bigrc_other@@ ReLU, $x^2$ or $x^2{+}x$ networks has such a range check, and it explains why parameter-heavy but MAC-light networks are set by their constants rather than their cells.  The rule is an empirical fit to settings we dumped, not a derivation from
ezkl's source, and our cost model predicts only the cell count (hence only the rows requirement).  $\varepsilon$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost} is reported
only through these residuals, and we do not claim an exact formula.
